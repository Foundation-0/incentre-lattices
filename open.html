Ledger

This page lists every claim made across the site, with the strength of its evidence and whether it is likely new. The short version: the drawing is a rediscovery of known structures by a new route, plus a handful of results that were not found in the literature after web searches. Nothing here has been through peer review, and no search was a full literature review. The proven results are written up in the preprint (PDF).

1How to read the labels

  • PROVEN an argument is given, or a symbolic computation checks it exactly.
  • VERIFIED NUMERICALLY computed in code to a stated tolerance, with no proof.
  • CONJECTURE proposed, tested only in part or not at all. Guesses and speculation go here too.
  • KNOWN in the literature, with a citation. Several are cited from abstracts and summaries only.
  • POSITION a philosophical or interpretive stance, argued for but not testable as stated (Drift and Search pages).

The second label on each row says whether the claim is likely new. ESTABLISHED means it is known. ELEMENTARY means it is easy to derive and probably folklore. NO REFERENCE FOUND means that web searches found no prior statement, which is weaker than “nobody has done this”. OUR ROUTE means the facts are known and only the path or the framing is ours.

3Candidate thesis questions

  1. The configuration space of the dot frameworks. Map the full configuration space of the ball and bar contact framework, as a real algebraic variety, for every Wythoff orbit. Which tilt paths are finite, where do they end, and is the incentre always the unique point whose folds end in a uniform packing? (Motion page.)
  2. The 3D fold beyond the cubic path. The cubic [111] fold stalls at density 0.4686, a waypoint; with shear the search reaches close packing on a 2×2×2 block. Test larger blocks and other stackings, replace the sampled continuity of the path by a proof that it is overlap-free, and map the other branches.
  3. Voronoi-cell Wythoff points as a generator of boron-rich frameworks. The cube gives CaB6 and the rhombic dodecahedron gives UB12. List the augmented nets of every face-transitive mirror-walled cell, treat α-boron as the frustrated dodecahedral case, and test with density functional theory whether the bond ratio (0.92 to 1.01 across the hexaborides and dodecaborides) follows electron count. (Matter page.)
  4. Where the radius-ratio rule can and cannot go. Test whether the shapes the rule cannot reach (7, 9 and dodecahedral 8 neighbours) are over-represented among oxide environments that violate Pauling’s first rule.
  5. The perceived centre of a triangle. Run the tap experiment with the incentre as a candidate, in adults and children, with tall and lopsided triangles. (Perception page.)
  6. A 3D contact rule for ornament. Apply a Hankin-style rule to the truncated cubic honeycomb, and teach the route from the drawing to Wythoff points as a workshop.

4The plain statement

This is a rediscovery of known structures by a new route. A drawing of a square, its diagonals and a dot in each triangle leads, by one rule about points equidistant from mirror walls, to the 4.8.8 tiling, the truncated cubic honeycomb, chemistry’s three radius-ratio limits and the calcium hexaboride structure Pauling described in 1934. Each of those is known. A few results were not found in the literature after web searches: the exact fold of 4.8.8 into the square packing and its 3D analogue, the mode and self-stress counts and the Poisson-ratio formula in n dimensions, the census of 159 frameworks (70 non-trivial after deduplication), the dodecaboride from the same rule, and the closed form of the packing peak. They may be folklore, they are mostly elementary, and none has been checked by a specialist. The search method was web search only.

The ledger was assembled from the evidence labels in the research reports. Where a report said “no reference found” it stays that and is not upgraded here.
