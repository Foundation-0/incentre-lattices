# Culture, cognition, education: the incentre-in-a-square drawing

Labels: KNOWN = from a source I read or searched (link given); VERIFIED = I computed it in code here (research/culture/gen.py, centres.py); SPECULATIVE = my inference. Caveat: web search returned summaries, several PDFs (Kaplan 2005, PMC) would not parse, so "KNOWN" items are from abstracts/snippets unless stated.

## 1. What the drawing is, structurally (VERIFIED)
- Tile the drawing (side-2 squares, X, incentre dots, dot-to-corner lines). Each dot sits at (1, sqrt2-1) in its triangle. Joining dots at distance 2(sqrt2-1) gives a regular 4.8.8: every interior dot has degree 3 (gen.py check). Images 01, 02, 05.
- The square+X alone is the **tetrakis square tiling**, the Catalan dual of 4.8.8. So the drawing is the tetrakis tiling with its own dual laid on top, with the dual's vertices at triangle incentres (not centroids; the dual vertex "face centre" is a free choice, incentre is the one that makes 4.8.8 regular). (VERIFIED; the dual relation itself is KNOWN standard.)
- The dot-to-corner lines are the triangle's angle bisectors (property of the incentre). In the 4.8.8 view they are the vertex-to-centre spokes of every octagon and small square (image 05). At an octagon centre you therefore see 16 rays: 8 axes plus 8 vertex spokes, the full D8 mirror set of a regular octagon, though the tiling's own group (p4m) only has the 4 mirror directions. So the drawing carries a local 8-fold rosette that the lattice does not. (VERIFIED by construction; the framing is mine.)
- Octagon inradius = 1 = half the side, so the octagons are the tangent unit-circle packing at the square's corners. Circle-packing-driven star rosettes exist (Brewer, Zha, Neno, Bridges 2022, https://archive.bridgesmathart.org/2022/bridges2022-391.html, KNOWN), which is the closest literature neighbour.

## 2. Islamic patterns
- KNOWN: Hankin's "polygons in contact" (1925): rays leave each edge midpoint at a contact angle into both neighbouring polygons, cut where they meet; Kaplan 2005 formalised it (https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~csk/publications/Papers/kaplan_2005.pdf, https://graphicsinterface.org/proceedings/gi2005/gi2005-22). Hankin's grid method is described at Bridges 2008 (https://archive.bridgesmathart.org/2008/bridges2008-21.html).
- Is the construction a Hankin variant? **No, I found nothing identical.** Hankin rays start at edge midpoints and are parameterised by angle; the construction's lines start at the incentre and go to corners (bisectors). It is a different decoration: a "centre-to-vertex" subdivision of a tiling, closer to a triangulation/mirror-line drawing than to a star pattern. I did not find it as a named traditional construction. (Absence of evidence only: my searches were limited and the key PDF unreadable.)
- Generated (VERIFIED code, not verified against history): `03_*` Hankin PIC on 4.8.8 at 22.5/30/45/60/67.5 deg; `07_sheet_4.8.8.svg` sweeps 12 angles; `04_*`, `08_sheet_tetrakis.svg` apply the same ray rule to the 45-45-90 tetrakis triangles. Observations: 4.8.8 at ~45 deg gives cross-shaped motifs around squares with octagons left as voids; at 67.5-78 deg an 8-point star-shaped void appears in each octagon with four-petal motifs in the squares; on the tetrakis triangles, theta=67.5 gives isolated regular octagons (a curiosity, probably an artefact of my simple adjacent-ray cut). I could NOT confirm that any frame is a specific named historical pattern (girih, Mamluk, jali, kumiko). SPECULATIVE: the 4.8.8 family is the standard base of eight-fold "star and cross"-type work, and someone with Kaplan's/Tilings catalogue could match frames properly. Next step: compare against Kaplan's Islamic Star Patterns gallery (https://static1.bridgesmathart.org/art-exhibits/bridges2001/kaplan/index.html) or Sakkal's corpus.
- 3D: KNOWN that 3D Islamic ornament is muqarnas; lifting 2D tessellations to muqarnas by algorithm is still open (Bridges 2025, https://archive.bridgesmathart.org/2025/bridges2025-145.html). I found no literature on space-filling 3D star lattices from Wythoff points. SPECULATIVE and attractive: truncated cubic honeycomb (cubes + truncated octahedra... ) fed through a 3D "contact" rule (polyhedra in contact, planes from face midpoints) is an untried-looking route; not searched exhaustively.

## 3. Cognition
- KNOWN: Dehaene, Izard, Pica, Spelke 2006 (Science 311:381): Munduruku adults and children from age 6 show core geometric intuitions (66.8% mean on odd-one-out; 39 of 43 concepts later reviewed, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.13281; summary https://news.mongabay.com/2006/01/indigenous-amazonians-display-core-understanding-of-geometry/). So untrained geometric intuition is expected; a child finding bisector/equidistance structure is not mysterious, but nobody tested incentres there.
- KNOWN: perceived centre of shapes. Studies find the perceived centre of polygons nearer the area barycentre than other classical centres (Sofia/Vicario line; https://ane.pl/index.php/ane/article/view/967; Yakimoff et al. for quadrangles); Proffitt 1982 found boundary contour dominates (https://uva.theopenscholar.com/dennis-proffitt/publications/roles-contour-and-luminance-distribution-determining-perceived-centers); Gatti 1924 reported upward displacement for equilateral triangles (second-hand). Those tested only a couple of isosceles triangles and, as far as the abstracts show, never included the incentre as a candidate.
- KNOWN: the medial axis/skeleton matters perceptually. Kovacs and Julesz found contrast enhancement at medial points (Nature 1994; Vision Res 1998, https://ruccs.rutgers.edu/images/archive/tech_rpt/tr-33/VisRes98.pdf); Firestone and Scholl 2014 and Ayzenberg et al. 2019 show tap locations cluster on the skeleton (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6894409; details unverified, page blocked).
- VERIFIED link: for a triangle the medial axis is the three angle bisectors, whose branch point is the incentre (= Chebyshev centre = pole of inaccessibility, the "visual centre" used in cartography/UI code; cf. patent https://patents.justia.com/patent/20190012571). So the three lines from the dot are literally the medial axis of the triangle. SPECULATIVE: drawing them traces the skeleton the visual system already computes.
- **Experiment (looks untried; not found in my search):** Participants click "the middle" of triangles. Candidates: centroid, incentre, circumcentre, orthocentre, bounding-box centre. Choose stimuli where they separate. VERIFIED separation (centres.py, image 09), incentre-to-centroid distance as % of perimeter: equilateral 0, right isosceles 1.7, 120-degree isosceles 1.8, 3-4-5 2.8, tall isosceles (1:3) **8.1**. The drawing's own right isosceles is a poor discriminator (1.7%), so use tall/lopsided triangles. Also compare tap-anywhere (Firestone-Scholl style) versus "click the centre", and children 5-10 versus adults, with the Munduruku-style non-verbal framing ("where would you hide a coin so it is equally far from all sides?" vs "balance point"). Cheap: online, ~60 participants per cell. Predictions: centre-click tracks centroid in adults for solid shapes (prior work); tap-anywhere shifts toward incentre/skeleton; physical balance instructions move to centroid. A positive incentre finding would be new.
- Children's drawing (recalled, UNVERIFIED): the diagonal cross inside a square is a late-developing item on copying tests (oblique effect). Worth checking; it would make a spontaneous X + bisectors drawing more remarkable.

## 4. Education
- KNOWN: kaleidoscope/Wythoff teaching exists: Palmer, "Pieces of Pi? Polyhedra, Orthoschemes and Dihedral Kaleidoscopes" (Bridges 2011, https://static1.bridgesmathart.org/2011/cdrom/proceedings/73/paper_73.pdf); Bridges 2015 kaleidoscope workshop (https://archive.bridgesmathart.org/2015/bridges2015-573.html); Freire's vZome Wythoff page (https://www3.mpifr-bonn.mpg.de/staff/pfreire/polyhedra/wythoff.htm). Wythoff generator point = equidistant from the mirrors it does not lie on (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wythoff_construction).
- Not found: any curriculum that starts from "put a dot at the incentre of each triangle of a square" and reaches Wythoff and crystal structures. SPECULATIVE pathway: (1) draw the figure; (2) discover 4.8.8 appears; (3) ask why the dot is tan 22.5 deg away; (4) mirror lines and symmetry groups; (5) same in 3D, radius ratios 0.225/0.414/0.732 as chemistry link; (6) Hankin rays as the art side. Low-floor, high-ceiling; a Bridges workshop paper fits.

## 5. Other threads (SPECULATIVE, unresearched)
Rhythm: sqrt2-1 = [0;2,2,2,...] is the silver ratio family (Pell), the shortest-period "metallic" case after the golden mean; a 4.8.8 timeline (edge ratio 1 : 1+sqrt2) is a ready-made polyrhythm for a playful teaching demo. Textiles/games: 4.8.8 is the standard "octagon and square" floor/quilt pattern; chess-style boards on it are known as a variant board (not checked).

## Ranking
1. Incentre = medial-axis branch point; the three lines are the triangle's skeleton; perceived-centre experiment with incentre candidate looks untried (hypothesis plus design above).
2. Drawing = tetrakis tiling plus dual 4.8.8 with local D8 rosette, unlike Hankin's midpoint rays (not found as a named traditional construction).
